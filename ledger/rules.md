| LZ-002 | `force` / postfix `!` act as a boundary of call-by-need evaluation | Observed in Go CLI (only the forms below) | CLI at the pinned SHA (with type annotations). Lazy side: unused argument `pick(42, 1 / 0)` → `42` (`lazy-unused`); unused binding `x = 1 / 0` → execution continues, exit 0 (`lazy-unused-binding`). Forced side: `pick(42, (1 / 0)!)` and `pick(42, force (1 / 0))` both give exit 1 even for an unused argument, with stderr `Runtime Error: <CASE>:4: division by zero at col 19` / `col 25` (`force-postfix-unused-arg` / `force-prefix-unused-arg`); `x = (1 / 0)!` gives exit 1 just by binding (`force-postfix-unused-binding`). `a = (10 + 20)!`, `force (30 + 40)`, `force 50 + 60` give `30 70 110` (`force-basic`). That is, a forcing expression is evaluated immediately at its evaluation position, and the lazy contrast changes just by removing `force` / `!` from the same expression. Source: `TestForceBuiltinAndPostfixOperator` in `pkg/eval/eval_test.go` (also re-confirmed via the CLI path with type annotations) | When the target of `force` is a tuple, dict, or method call (sections 5–6 of the same test); the effect of `force` on memoization of return values; precedence of `force` (why `force 50 + 60` is 110). | AST-001 | Prefix `force` applies only to the immediately following term (an atomic expression such as `50`, or a parenthesized expression), binding at least tighter than `+`. Postfix `!` and prefix `force` produce the same `Force` node | Observed in Go parser (`lesdump`) | With the parser at the pinned SHA, `c = force 50 + 60` is `Binary(+, Force(Int 50), Int 60)`, i.e. `(force 50) + 60` (`lesdump/golden/force-basic.json`). `force (30 + 40)` and `(10 + 20)!` are both `Force(Binary(+, …))`. For the same `force (1 / 0)` / `(1 / 0)!`, the `pos` of `Force` differs (prefix at the keyword, col 16; postfix at `!`, col 23). This explains the CLI observation `c = 110` (`force-basic`): only `50` is forced, and `+ 60` is ordinary addition. Source: `pkg/parser` (not read; observation only) | Binding of `force` with other binary operators, comparisons, and unary operators; `force force x`; chains of `!`. We do not aim for full syntax coverage |
